Causal Inference · Quasi-Experimental Methods · Policy Evaluation

Causal Inference & Policy Evaluation

The methodological foundation behind the portfolio. Applied causal inference across public policy, social protection, energy investment evaluation, and marketplace analytics — answering the question that dashboards cannot: did this intervention actually cause the outcome, or would it have happened anyway?

Difference-in-Differences
Panel data evaluation of policy interventions. Applied across social protection programmes, marketplace experiments, and energy investment analysis.
Synthetic Control
Counterfactual estimation for single treated units. Used in marketplace intervention evaluation and regional programme assessment.
Propensity Score Matching
Selection bias correction for observational studies. Applied in social protection targeting evaluation and marketplace user analysis.
Instrumental Variables
Endogeneity correction for non-random treatment selection. Applied in marketplace programme evaluation and energy investment analysis.
Event Study
Pre-trend validation and dynamic treatment effect estimation. Used for parallel trends testing and intervention ramp-up analysis.
CUPED & Bayesian A/B
Variance reduction and probabilistic decision frameworks for marketplace experimentation. Reduces required sample size by 30–50%.

These methods are applied across all four portfolio domains — from evaluating whether a social protection programme reached the right households, to estimating the true causal impact of a marketplace intervention, to auditing whether an AI eligibility system produces unbiased outcomes across population subgroups. See the Causal Inference & Incrementality Engine for a production demonstration.

About the Work

Built for Decisions That Cannot Wait for Perfect Evidence

Every tool in this portfolio was built to answer one question: how can institutions make better decisions when evidence is incomplete, resources are constrained, and uncertainty is unavoidable?

I have spent 14 years building frameworks for exactly that situation — as Founder and Executive Director of Poverty 360, leading programs reaching 58,000+ direct and indirect beneficiaries across Ghana, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, and the United States; as an applied economist trained in Development Economics, Energy Economics, Resource Economics, and Econometrics; and as a researcher examining how AI-assisted decision systems create representation failures that exclude the most vulnerable populations from public services.
